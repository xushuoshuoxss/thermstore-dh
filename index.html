Summer heat,
saved for winter.

Data centres and industries produce heat that often goes unused, especially in summer when heating demand is low. ThermStore-DH explores how to store this surplus heat underground and use it to warm homes and buildings in winter.

How it works Our Research
  1. 1 · Collect

    Capture surplus heat

    Data centres and industries produce heat that often goes unused, especially in summer.

  2. 2 · Store

    Store underground

    Surplus heat is transferred underground and stored in soil, rock or groundwater for months.

  3. 3 · Release

    Heat homes in winter

    The stored heat is recovered and delivered to homes, schools and buildings through district heating networks.

Why it matters

The heat is there.
Just not when we need it.

Homes need far more heat in winter than in summer. Yet data centres and industries produce heat all year round, and in summer most of it is simply released into the air.

  • 5.5×

    Higher residential gas demand in January than July

  • ~38%

  • 2.7 TWh

How it works

From underground storage to district heating

District heating

District heating delivers heat from an energy centre to homes and buildings through insulated underground pipes, replacing the need for individual boilers.

  • Hot water flows out to homes and buildings.
  • Cooler water returns to be heated again.
  • Underground storage saves surplus heat for use when demand is higher.

How underground heat storage works

Animations: in summer, heat flows down into the ground and the warm zone grows. In winter, the flow reverses and the stored heat is brought back up.

Which option fits depends on the ground below. Finding that out is part of our research.

Our Research

Making underground heat storage work for Ireland

ThermStore-DH brings together geoscience, engineering and energy planning to investigate how underground thermal energy storage can support the development of district heating in Ireland.

Understanding the ground

Investigating geological and groundwater conditions to identify suitable locations for underground heat storage.

Designing thermal storage

Studying heat transfer and storage performance to develop effective underground storage solutions.

Connecting to district heating

Assessing how underground storage can be integrated with district heating networks and surplus heat sources.

Supporting implementation

Working with stakeholders to identify opportunities and challenges for deployment in Ireland.
