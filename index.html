Summer heat,
saved for winter.

Every summer, data centres, power plants and the sun produce heat that nobody needs, and most of it is simply thrown away. ThermStore-DH explores how to keep that spare heat in the ground beneath our cities for months, and bring it back to homes and buildings in winter through district heating.

Why it matters

The heat is there.
Just not when we need it.

Homes need far more heat in winter than in summer. Yet data centres and power plants produce heat all year round, and in summer most of it is simply released into the air.

  • 5.5×

    more heat is needed in January than in July

  • 24/7

    data centres and many power plants produce heat, day and night, all year round

Heat that goes to waste today

In summer, when homes need little heating, this heat has nowhere to go. Stored underground, it can be saved for winter.

Source: CSO, gas demand of homes and small businesses, monthly average 2019–2025.

District heating

How the heat reaches homes

District heating works like a water supply, but for heat. Instead of a boiler in every home, insulated pipes under the street carry hot water from an energy centre to homes and buildings across a neighbourhood.

  • Hot water flows out to homes, schools and offices.
  • Cooler water flows back to the energy centre to be heated again.
  • Many clean sources can feed one network: spare heat from industry and data centres, solar heat and heat pumps.

Where ThermStore-DH fits: we connect an underground heat store to the district heating network. In summer the network carries spare heat down into the store. In winter it brings that heat back to the buildings.

2.7 TWh

of district heating is targeted for Ireland by 2030. These networks will need large amounts of heat in winter, and somewhere to store it.

Source: Climate Action Plan.

Underground thermal energy storage

A season's worth of heat, stored underground

A district heating network needs a lot of heat in winter. The ground beneath our cities can hold enough of it, for months.

Big enough for district heating

A large volume of ground can hold heat for months: enough to meet the winter demand of a whole district heating network, not just one building.

Steady

A few metres down, the ground keeps a steady temperature all year round, a natural place to keep heat.

Out of sight

The store sits underground, beneath parks, sports grounds or car parks. The space above stays in everyday use.

Two ways to store heat underground

Animations: in summer, heat flows down into the ground and the warm zone grows. In winter, the flow reverses and the stored heat is brought back up.

Which option fits depends on the ground below. Finding that out is part of our research.

Impact

Less fuel burned. Lower emissions.

Most homes in Ireland are still heated by oil or gas. Every unit of spare summer heat used in winter is heat that does not have to come from burning fuel.

  • ~38%

    of Ireland's energy-related CO₂ comes from heating

  • Less fuel

    Stored heat replaces gas and oil burned in boilers.

  • Less waste

    Heat that is already produced gets used, instead of being released into the air.

Source: SEAI.
